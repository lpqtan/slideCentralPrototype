CPF Housing Usage — Key Policy Details (as at 2025)

1. Eligible uses of CPF Ordinary Account (OA) for HDB flats:
   - Down payment (minimum 5% cash for HDB loan; 20% cash if using bank loan)
   - Monthly mortgage instalments (HDB loan or bank loan)
   - Buyer's Stamp Duty (BSD) and Additional Buyer's Stamp Duty (ABSD) where applicable
   - Legal fees and HDB conveyancing fees

2. Withdrawal limits:
   - Valuation Limit (VL): Maximum CPF that can be used = lower of purchase price
     or market valuation
   - Withdrawal Limit (WL): Once CPF usage hits 120% of VL, members must set aside
     the Full Retirement Sum (FRS) in OA+SA before using more CPF
   - Members aged 55+ must have FRS set aside before withdrawing for housing

3. Accrued interest:
   - CPF withdrawn for housing accrues interest at OA rate (currently 2.5% p.a.)
   - Accrued interest must be returned to CPF upon sale of property (not to another party)
   - Accrued interest can significantly reduce cash proceeds from resale — e.g. a member
     who withdrew S$200,000 in CPF over 25 years may owe S$120,000+ in accrued interest
     back to CPF upon selling

4. Retirement adequacy impact:
   - Heavy CPF housing drawdown can leave members with insufficient retirement savings
   - CPF Board encourages members to use cash for housing where possible to preserve
     OA balance for retirement
   - Right-sizing flat choice matters — a 4-room flat vs 5-room flat can mean
     S$100,000–S$200,000 difference in total CPF usage over a 25-year loan

5. Common member misconceptions:
   - "I can use all my CPF for housing" — No. Valuation Limit and Withdrawal Limit cap usage.
   - "The money I put in doesn't need to come back" — Accrued interest must be refunded on sale.
   - "I can use my SA for housing" — SA cannot be used for housing; only OA applies.
   - "Accrued interest is paid to the bank" — No. It is returned to the member's own CPF account.

6. Key figures for context:
   - OA interest rate: 2.5% p.a. (guaranteed)
   - Median HDB resale price (4-room, 2025): approx. S$550,000
   - Typical CPF usage over a 25-year HDB loan: S$180,000–S$320,000 depending on loan size
   - Members with insufficient CPF for FRS at 55 due to housing withdrawals: est. 1 in 4

Scam Incident Data — Internal Briefing Pack (Q3 FY2025)

INCIDENT SUMMARY
- Total CPF-impersonation scam cases reported to SPF: 1,847 in Q3 FY2025
  (+38% vs Q3 FY2024)
- Estimated financial losses to members: S$14.2m in Q3 FY2025
  (vs S$9.1m same period last year)
- Most affected demographic: members aged 50–65 (41% of cases),
  followed by 35–49 (32%)
- Peak scam activity: Fridays 4pm–8pm and Saturday mornings
  (consistent with scammer call-centre shift patterns)
- Average financial loss per victim: S$7,700 (up from S$5,900 in Q3 FY2024)

ATTACK VECTORS BREAKDOWN
1. Fake SPF / CPF officer phone calls (55% of cases)
   - Scammers claim member's CPF account has been "flagged for illegal activity"
   - Instruct victim to transfer CPF funds to a "safe account" or reveal OTP
   - Use caller ID spoofing to display official CPF Board number (+65 1800-227-1188)
   - Calls typically last 45–90 minutes; victims kept on the line to prevent them
     seeking a second opinion

2. Phishing SMS (30% of cases)
   - SMS with link claiming member has "unclaimed CPF credits" or
     "account verification required"
   - Links lead to fake CPF website that harvests SingPass credentials
   - 12 unique phishing domains identified and taken down by IMDA in Q3
   - Average time from domain registration to takedown: 11 days (too slow)

3. Fraudulent withdrawal requests (15% of cases)
   - Insider-assisted or SIM-swap cases where scammer has member's full personal data
   - Submits CPF withdrawal request and intercepts OTP via SIM swap
   - Average loss per case: S$38,000 — the highest loss category

CURRENT CONTROLS AND GAPS
- CPF Board sends in-app notification for all withdrawal requests — but only 23% of
  members have CPF mobile app installed
- OTP validity window is currently 10 minutes — industry standard moving to 3 minutes
- No outbound call capability to warn members before large withdrawals are processed
- Member reporting lag: average 4.7 days before victim reports to SPF
  (funds typically transferred overseas within 2 hours of theft)

PEER AGENCY RESPONSES
- IRAS: Implemented 24-hour cooling period for first-time bank account changes;
  reduced fraud losses by 61% in 6 months
- HDB: Added mandatory video call verification for large e-payment transactions
- MAS: ScamShield integration for all major bank apps — CPF app not yet integrated

THREE PROPOSED OPTIONS

Option A — Hardened OTP and Transaction Controls (cost: S$0.8m, timeline: 3 months)
  - Reduce OTP validity window from 10 minutes to 3 minutes
  - Introduce 6-hour cooling period for new payee additions
  - Block withdrawals to bank accounts registered less than 72 hours ago
  - Send SMS alert to member's registered mobile before any withdrawal above S$5,000
  Expected impact: eliminate ~60% of fraudulent withdrawal cases

Option B — Accelerated Public Awareness Campaign (cost: S$2.1m, timeline: 6 weeks)
  - Mass SMS blast to all 4.2m active members with scam warning and verification tips
  - CPF spokesperson media appearances (CNA, Lianhe Zaobao, Tamil Murasu, Berita Harian)
  - Pop-up alerts on CPF website and mobile app for 90 days
  - Partner with community centres for roadshows targeting members aged 50–65
  Expected impact: reduce victim rate among aware members by ~40%; slower to show effect

Option C — Joint Enforcement Task Force with SPF (cost: S$0.5m staff time, ongoing)
  - Dedicated joint team to fast-track investigation of reported cases
  - Real-time data sharing between CPF Board and SPF for case triage
  - Target: reduce case-to-arrest timeline from current 47 days to under 14 days
  - Deter scam syndicates through higher arrest visibility
  Expected impact: deterrence effect; limited near-term victim reduction

RECOMMENDED APPROACH: Option A + Option C in parallel (combined ~S$1.3m),
with Option B as Phase 2 if Q4 volumes remain elevated above 1,500 cases/month.

MediSave Usage Reference Guide — Staff Training Edition (FY2025)

WHAT IS MEDISAVE
- MediSave is the healthcare savings component of CPF, separate from OA and SA
- Employee contributions: 8%–10.5% of monthly wages, depending on age
  (under 35: 8%; 35–44: 9%; 45–49: 9.5%; 50–55: 10.5%)
- Basic Healthcare Sum (BHS) FY2025: S$75,500 — balances above BHS flow to SA or RA
- Interest rate: 4% p.a. (same as SA), guaranteed by the government
- Unused MediSave can be used for immediate family members (spouse, children, parents,
  grandparents) who are Singapore Citizens or Permanent Residents

APPROVED USES

1. Hospitalisation and Day Surgery
   - Covers ward charges, surgical fees, anaesthesiologist fees, medical investigations
     during a hospital admission
   - Daily withdrawal limits by ward class:
     C-class: S$450/day | B2: S$650/day | B1 and above: S$900/day
   - No annual cap on hospitalisation — only limited by MediSave balance
   - Integrated Shield Plan (IP) hospital insurance premiums: MediSave can pay the
     basic MediShield Life component; riders must be paid cash

2. Outpatient Chronic Disease Management Programme (CDMP)
   - Covers treatment for 24 MOH-approved chronic conditions including diabetes,
     hypertension, high cholesterol, asthma, schizophrenia, and major depression
   - Withdrawal limit: S$500/year per patient at polyclinics
     (or S$700/year if patient is seen at a CHAS GP clinic)
   - Can be used for immediate family members at the same annual cap
   - Members must check that their clinic is CDMP-accredited before assuming coverage

3. Vaccinations — MediSave 700 Scheme
   - Covers MOH National Childhood Immunisation Schedule (NCIS) and approved
     adult flexi-vaccinations
   - Annual cap: S$700 per MediSave account (shared across all approved vaccinations)
   - Approved adult vaccines include: flu, pneumococcal, HPV, hepatitis B, varicella
   - Vaccination must be administered at an approved clinic to qualify

4. MediShield Life Premiums
   - MediSave can pay the full MediShield Life premium for the member and their dependants
   - Premium amounts are tiered by age band — published annually by MOH
   - Rider premiums (for Integrated Shield Plans) cannot use MediSave; cash only
   - CareShield Life and ElderShield supplement premiums: also payable via MediSave

5. Maternity and Delivery Charges
   - S$900 per confinement, up to 3 confinements per lifetime (per MediSave account)
   - Covers pre-delivery check-ups, ultrasounds, delivery charges, and post-natal
     inpatient stays
   - Father's MediSave can also be used for spouse's delivery

COMMON MISCONCEPTIONS STAFF ENCOUNTER (and correct responses)

Q: "Can I use MediSave for dental?"
A: Only for hospitalisation resulting from dental surgery (e.g. jaw surgery under GA).
   Routine dental scaling, fillings, and extractions at dental clinics are NOT covered.

Q: "Can I withdraw MediSave as cash if I don't need it for medical expenses?"
A: No. MediSave cannot be withdrawn as cash at any age — it is ring-fenced for healthcare.
   (Exception: upon death, MediSave balance is distributed to nominees/beneficiaries.)

Q: "Does my MediSave expire if I don't use it?"
A: No. MediSave does not expire. Unused balances accrue 4% interest annually
   and can be passed to immediate family members for their approved medical expenses.

Q: "Can I use my child's MediSave for my own hospitalisation?"
A: No. MediSave usage flows from parent to child (or member to dependant), not reverse.
   A child cannot authorise their own MediSave for the parent's treatment.

Q: "My MediSave balance is above S$75,500. Do I lose the extra money?"
A: No. The excess above the Basic Healthcare Sum (BHS) flows automatically into your
   Special Account (if under 55) or Retirement Account (if above 55). It is not lost.

TOP 5 MEMBER CALL CENTRE QUESTIONS (FY2025 call data, n=18,400 MediSave calls)

1. "How do I check my MediSave balance?"
   → Direct to CPF online portal (my.cpf.gov.sg) or CPF mobile app → My CPF → MediSave

2. "Can I use MediSave at a private clinic?"
   → Yes, but only for CDMP-approved treatments at CDMP-accredited private clinics.
     For all other uses, must be at a hospital or registered vaccination clinic.

3. "Why was my MediSave claim rejected?"
   → Most common reasons: (a) treatment not on approved list, (b) clinic not accredited,
     (c) annual cap already reached, (d) treatment claimed under wrong scheme code

4. "Can I use MediSave for my elderly parent's hospitalisation?"
   → Yes, if the parent is a Singapore Citizen or PR. Submit via CPF portal under
     "Immediate Family Member Claim."

5. "How much can I use per year?"
   → There is no single annual cap across all categories. Limits vary:
     hospitalisation (no cap), CDMP (S$500–S$700/year), vaccinations (S$700/year).
     Large hospitalisation bills are limited only by MediSave balance.

CPF LIFE Payout Adequacy Review — EXCO Briefing Data Pack (Q4 FY2025)

CURRENT STATE: PAYOUT DISTRIBUTION
- CPF LIFE launched in 2009; currently 780,000+ members receiving CPF LIFE payouts
- Median monthly payout (Standard Plan, age 65): S$1,380/month
- Bottom 30% monthly payout range: S$680–S$980/month
- Basic Retirement Standard (MOF benchmark for basic standard of living, 2025):
  S$1,379/month for a single elderly person
- Gap for bottom 30% of members: S$400–S$700/month below basic retirement standard
- Projected to worsen: without intervention, bottom 30% gap widens to S$500–S$900/month
  by 2030 due to ageing cohort demographics

CPF LIFE PLAN DISTRIBUTION (as at December 2025)
- Standard Plan (higher monthly payouts, smaller bequest): 71% of members
- Basic Plan (lower monthly payouts, larger bequest): 24% of members
- Escalating Plan (inflation-adjusted payouts, starts lower): 5% of members
- Default plan for members who did not choose: Standard Plan (since 2016)

RETIREMENT SUM TIERS AND CORRESPONDING PAYOUTS (2025 figures)
- Basic Retirement Sum (BRS): S$106,500 → approx. S$830–S$900/month payout at 65
- Full Retirement Sum (FRS): S$213,000 → approx. S$1,570–S$1,700/month payout at 65
- Enhanced Retirement Sum (ERS): S$426,000 → approx. S$2,500–S$2,700/month payout at 65
- Members reaching FRS at 55: 48% of active members
- Members reaching only BRS at 55: 31% of active members
- Members below BRS at 55: 21% of active members (most vulnerable cohort)

ROOT CAUSE ANALYSIS

1. Low-wage contribution gap (primary driver, est. 55% of explained variance)
   - Members earning below S$2,000/month contribute S$400–S$600/month less in CPF
     than median earners over a working lifetime
   - A 40-year contribution gap compounds to S$150,000–S$200,000 difference in
     Retirement Account (RA) balance at 65, even before housing withdrawals

2. Contribution disruptions (secondary driver, est. 25% of explained variance)
   - Average Singaporean has 4.2 years of zero or minimal CPF contribution during
     working life (career gaps, caregiving leave, self-employment periods, illness)
   - Each contribution-free year reduces CPF LIFE payout by approx. S$35–S$50/month
     at retirement due to forgone compounding

3. Early and heavy housing withdrawals (tertiary driver, est. 20% of explained variance)
   - Members who use the maximum CPF allowable for housing have 28% lower RA balances
     at 55 compared to members who use cash for housing
   - OA-to-RA transfer at 55 partially mitigates this but cannot recover
     compounding lost interest on 20–25 years of withdrawn OA funds

THREE POLICY OPTIONS FOR CONSIDERATION

Option 1 — Enhanced Voluntary Top-Up Incentives (MOF/CPF joint funding)
  Mechanism: Government co-matches voluntary cash top-ups to RA at S$0.50 per S$1.00
             topped up, capped at S$3,000/year (i.e. max government co-contribution S$1,500/year)
  Target: Incentivise 200,000 low-income members (bottom 40% by CPF balance)
          to top up S$1,000–S$2,000/year
  Estimated cost to government: S$100m–S$150m/year at full programme scale
  Projected payout increase for participants: S$120–S$180/month at 65
  Timeline to implementation: 18 months (requires Budget 2026 announcement and legislation)
  Key risk: Uptake may be low among members most in need (behavioural inertia)

Option 2 — Employer Retirement Contribution Nudge
  Mechanism: Amend CPF Act to allow (not mandate) employers to make voluntary
             top-ups to employees' RA as part of total compensation
             Tax incentive: employer RA top-ups deductible at 150% of contribution amount
  Target employers: SMEs with >50 employees in low-wage sectors
                   (food & beverage, cleaning, retail, security)
  Projected coverage: 85,000 workers in first 2 years at 30% employer adoption rate
  Timeline: 12 months (legislative amendment to CPF Act required)
  Key risk: SMEs may resist additional compensation costs even with tax incentive

Option 3 — BRS Recalibration and Drawdown Flexibility (quickest to implement)
  Mechanism A: Raise BRS to S$130,000 (phased over 3 years, +S$8,000/year)
               to reflect updated retirement cost data from MOF's 2025 spending survey
  Mechanism B: Allow members with FRS or above to defer CPF LIFE payouts from 65 to 70
               — deferred payouts are approx. 30% higher per month
  Mechanism C: Introduce one-time partial lump-sum withdrawal of up to S$10,000 at 65
               without reducing monthly LIFE payout amount
  Timeline: 6 months (no legislative change required — policy instrument only)
  Key risk: BRS increase may create public perception of "moving goalposts"

WORKING GROUP RECOMMENDATION
Pursue Option 3 immediately (6 months) to address urgent adequacy gap and signal action.
Announce Option 1 in Budget 2026 for medium-term impact.
Commission feasibility study on Option 2 for 2027 Budget consideration.
Combined estimated payout improvement for bottom 30%: +S$250–S$380/month by FY2028.

SUCCESS METRICS (2-year horizon)
- Primary: Bottom 30% average monthly payout increases from S$830 to S$1,080+
- Secondary: Members reaching FRS at 55 increases from 48% to 55%
- Tertiary: Voluntary top-up participation rate among target cohort reaches 25%

SEP CPF Contribution Gap — CMM Budget Paper Background (November 2025)

WHO ARE SELF-EMPLOYED PERSONS (SEPs)?
- SEPs include: freelancers, sole proprietors, commission-based agents (insurance,
  property), gig platform workers (Grab, Gojek, Lalamove, Deliveroo, Shopee Food),
  and independent contractors
- Estimated SEP population in Singapore: 225,000 (MOM Labour Force Survey 2025)
- Growing segment: SEP workforce grew 18% from 2020–2025, driven primarily by
  gig economy platform expansion
- Median annual net trade income (NTI) of SEPs: S$28,000
  (vs S$55,000 for full-time employees)
- Age distribution: 38% are aged 30–45 (the critical savings-building window)

CPF RULES FOR SEPs
- MediSave contributions: Mandatory for SEPs earning above S$6,000 NTI/year
  Rate: 8%–10.5% of NTI depending on age (same rate as employees)
- OA and SA contributions: Entirely voluntary for SEPs
  No employer contribution; no mandatory employee OA or SA deduction
- Key difference from employees: An employee earning S$3,000/month accumulates
  approx. S$17,280/year in combined CPF contributions (employee + employer)
  A SEP earning the same income contributes only S$2,520/year (MediSave only)
  — a gap of S$14,760/year

THE RETIREMENT ADEQUACY GAP: SEP vs EMPLOYEE

By age 55:
- Median CPF balance for an employee:   S$298,000
- Median CPF balance for a SEP:         S$142,000
- Gap:                                  S$156,000

CPF LIFE monthly payout projection at 65:
- Median employee payout:               S$1,380/month
- Median SEP payout:                    S$780/month
- Gap:                                  S$600/month

Retirement Sum achievement at 55:
- Employees reaching Full Retirement Sum (FRS): 48%
- SEPs reaching FRS:                            18%
- SEPs below Basic Retirement Sum (BRS):        47%

Voluntary contribution rate:
- Only 42% of SEPs contribute anything beyond the mandatory MediSave minimum
- Of those who do contribute voluntarily, median annual voluntary amount: S$2,800

BARRIERS TO VOLUNTARY CONTRIBUTION (2025 SEP Sentiment Survey, n=1,200)
1. Irregular income — 68% of respondents
   "I don't know how much to set aside when my income is unpredictable month-to-month."
2. Low awareness of voluntary contribution options — 54%
   "I didn't know I could contribute to my OA or SA voluntarily."
3. Preference for liquid emergency savings — 47%
   "I need cash accessible for business expenses. I can't afford to lock money in CPF."
4. Complexity of CPF portal for SEPs — 38%
   "It's confusing to fill in net trade income versus gross income fields."
5. Distrust in CPF lock-in and payout age — 29%
   "My money will be locked until I'm 55 or 65. I'd rather invest it myself."

  Mechanism: Default suggested contribution = 10% of NTI, presented as a split

  Expected conversion: 15% of nudged SEPs make at least one voluntary contribution

  Timeline: Requires IRAS and CPF API integration; 4 months lead time

Component 2 — Financial Literacy Webinar Series (Budget: S$200,000)
  Format: 12 webinars across 12 months, 45 minutes each, conducted online
